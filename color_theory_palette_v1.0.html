How light adds

Every light adds its own wavelengths, so overlaps get brighter. Screens, stage lights and projectors mix color this way.

+=Yellow
+=Cyan
+=Magenta
++=White

Your retina has three kinds of cone cell, most sensitive to long (red), medium (green) and short (blue) wavelengths. Red light plus green light stimulates the L and M cones the same way pure yellow light does. You see yellow even though no yellow wavelength is there.

Try it: switch Vision to Deutan. The red and green lights become hard to tell apart, because the M cones that separate them are missing.

How pigment subtracts

Inks and filters work by absorbing light. White paper reflects every color, and each ink removes one. Each overlap removes more, so overlaps get darker.

All three together should give black. Real inks absorb unevenly and give a muddy dark brown, so printers add a separate black ink. That is why printing uses CMYK.

Try it: change the light to Red. The paper looks red, and magenta and yellow ink disappear into it, since both reflect red. Only cyan, which absorbs red, shows up, as black.

Reading the wheel

A color wheel puts hues in a circle, so relationships between colors become shapes: a line, a triangle, a rectangle.

RYB is the painters' wheel, with primaries red, yellow and blue. Complements are red–green, yellow–violet and blue–orange. RGB is the wheel for light, with primaries red, green and blue. There the complements are red–cyan, green–magenta and blue–yellow, and two complementary lights add up to white.

Check the palette in Grayscale. Two colors that become the same gray differ only in hue. A reader with color blindness, or a black-and-white printout, can lose that difference.
