    The physics
    k = E·A / Laxial stiffness of a member
    P_cr = π²·E·I / L²Euler buckling load
    M = E·I·κbending moment from curvature κ
    use = |F|/F_cap + |M|/M_capmember breaks above 100%
    ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0at every joint, when at rest
    m + r ≥ 2jpin = 2, footing = 3; a continuous joint adds 1 to m
    q = ½·ρ·v²·C_dwind pressure on a 0.3 m-wide face

    Every joint you place is a pin: it lets members rotate freely, which is why a square folds into a parallelogram while a triangle holds its shape. Members themselves are simulated as chains of ~1 m pieces with bending stiffness, so a long beam sags under its own weight and bends under loads placed along it. Red = pulled (tension), blue = pushed (compression), amber = mostly bending. Long members in compression bow sideways and buckle well below their crushing strength. Cables are treated as straight, tight lines that carry tension only and go slack (dashed) when pushed. When you attach a member to the middle of a beam, a new joint (green square) is made there and the beam stays continuous through it, so it still resists bending; use “Make it a hinge” to cut that continuity. Concrete pillars are the other exception to pinned joints: where a pillar meets the ground its footing grips it rigidly, so the base resists bending like a tree trunk.
